# When Financial Infrastructure Stops Sleeping

**SINTELRA-Public**
**07 September 2026**

**Focus:** Cross-border Payments • Settlement Time • Availability • Financial Infrastructure • Continuous Capacity

## Public Question

**What changes when financial infrastructure can move money across borders without waiting for traditional banking hours?**

For much of modern finance, payment infrastructure has had a clock.

A payment instruction may be created electronically at any time, but the infrastructure required to clear, fund, and finally settle that payment has often operated within defined windows. Different banking days, weekends, holidays, time zones, and settlement-system opening hours can therefore become part of the payment process itself.

The movement toward continuously available settlement changes this structure.

It turns **time from an operating constraint into usable infrastructure capacity**.

### Settlement Capacity Is Also Time Capacity

Financial infrastructure is often discussed in terms of transaction volumes, processing speed, or system throughput.

But availability is another form of capacity.

A settlement system capable of processing a large number of transactions during a limited operating window still has a temporal constraint. Payments arriving outside that window may need to wait for the infrastructure to reopen or for another settlement system in the payment chain to become available.

The Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures has identified differences in payment-system operating hours as one of the frictions affecting cross-border payments. Extending and aligning those hours can increase payment speed while reducing some liquidity costs and settlement risks. Full 24/7 operation represents the furthest extension of that model.

The structural change is therefore larger than moving a cutoff time.

The infrastructure itself becomes available for a greater proportion of the calendar.

### From Processing Windows to Continuous Availability

Elements of this model already exist.

The Eurosystem's TARGET Instant Payment Settlement platform, or TIPS, settles instant payments in central bank money around the clock, 365 days a year. Payments can be settled in real time rather than waiting for the next conventional banking session.

Cross-border architecture is also moving toward linking domestic instant-payment systems. BIS Project Nexus demonstrated a model in which interconnected instant-payment systems could process cross-border payments within 60 seconds in most cases. Following the project's development phases, central banks from India, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand established Nexus Global Payments in 2025 to move the architecture toward live implementation.

This does not mean that the world's cross-border settlement infrastructure is already universally available 24/7.

The transition remains uneven.

For example, the Bank of England is pursuing a phased extension of RTGS and CHAPS operating hours. An earlier opening is planned from September 2027, while the longer-term direction is toward near-24x7 settlement.

What is emerging is therefore not a single global switch from "closed" to "open."

It is a gradual expansion of the periods during which financial infrastructure can actually perform settlement.

### What Changes When the Waiting Period Shrinks?

The first change is straightforward: **payments no longer need to accumulate simply because infrastructure is unavailable.**

A company operating across Asia, Europe, and North America is less dependent on matching its activity to the banking calendar of every jurisdiction involved.

A payment initiated late in one region does not necessarily have to become tomorrow's settlement problem somewhere else.

But the more important effect appears deeper inside the financial system.

Continuous settlement can reduce the role of artificial waiting periods created by operating hours. That may improve liquidity management and reduce some settlement exposure because obligations can be completed closer to the time they arise rather than being held until another system opens.

In other words, financial infrastructure begins to resemble other continuously available network infrastructure.

The system is no longer designed only around the business day.

It is designed around the transaction.

### The Bottleneck Does Not Disappear

Continuous availability does not eliminate financial constraints.

It changes where they appear.

If the settlement platform is open but liquidity is unavailable, the payment can still be constrained.

If two national payment systems operate continuously but cannot interoperate, the border remains a barrier.

If foreign-exchange liquidity, sanctions screening, fraud controls, correspondent-bank processes, or participant systems remain dependent on restricted operating windows, the transaction may still encounter delay.

And when infrastructure operates continuously, maintenance, cyber resilience, incident response, liquidity management, and operational staffing must also adapt to an environment with fewer natural shutdown periods.

The infrastructure gains time capacity.

The operational burden becomes continuous as well.

### A Migration of Financial Friction

This is why the transition toward 24/7 cross-border settlement should not be understood simply as a story about faster payments.

It is a change in the location of financial friction.

Under a banking-hour-dependent system, one constraint is obvious:

**the infrastructure may simply be closed.**

As that constraint weakens, attention shifts toward other limits:

**interoperability, liquidity, foreign exchange, compliance, operational resilience, and access.**

The bottleneck moves.

That migration matters because it changes where institutions must build capacity next.

### SINTELRA Note

A financial system does not become continuously capable merely because a payment message can be transmitted at any hour.

Continuous financial infrastructure requires the systems necessary to complete the transaction-not only initiate it-to remain sufficiently available and interoperable.

The structural transition therefore runs from:

**banking-hour-dependent settlement**

toward

**continuously available settlement capacity.**

The significance is not that finance suddenly moves faster.

It is that an increasing part of the financial system no longer needs to wait for the clock before it can move at all.
